Maya

5 years ago, I used to live by a massive park, and I'd walk Maya every single day after school.

She was around 5 or 6 years old at this time, and had so much energy she would be impossible to contain. She was a Yorkie, mixed with something I have no idea what, because she was way bigger than a normal one.

The park had tons of birds that would sit around on the grass, and Maya learnt this phrase "Where are the birds?" in Lithuanian. Every time she'd hear it, she would stop completely, look over at me and wait for me to point over towards the birds. She would then start sprinting max speed towards the birds, barking and scaring them off. It didn't have to be birds exactly, but it worked better when there was something for her to chase after xd.

She remembered this phrase her whole life, and even when she got older she would still stop, wait, then run like hell to wherever you pointed. She would run back afterwards all happy n shit wagging her tail like she accomplished her task of being a nuisance. She was proud of it, and it made me happy.

The climber

I will become Mori incarnate.

Around a month ago i started hiking once or twice a week, recently i've been going every 2 to 3 days. I've also started scrambling way more. And holy shit it's the most fun ive had in years. All the places i've been to have been extremely exposed, like one slip i'll probably just die - but i feel confident and secure on my holds, climbing etc. Only thing i don't like is the environment where i live. Eastern Spain is just pine trees and shrubs there's no proper rainforests or something different, you'd have to drive over 2/3 hours to get somewhere slightly different. The mountains aren't too bad though.

I've noticed my fitness has improved dramatically, when i first started going on hikes i would be completely out of breath within a few minutes, my legs would be hurting and i'd have to take breaks and sit down. Only after around a month of this, the local mountain that once took me 6 or 7 breaks to walk up I can blitz it, only taking a break around the 90% mark because there onwards it's practically scrambling up loose rock and is tedious to climb up. I would always sit down when resting, for maybe 5 minutes at a time, but now all it takes is maybe 30 seconds (sometimes less) of standing letting my legs recover before i can carry on.

There is still tons i have to improve. Having done practically no cardio my whole life, my endurance is really poor and my heart isn't that strong. So while my muscles have adapted to hiking, my heart often gives out before they do, forcing me to rest when i would otherwise be able to carry on. I really hope this gets better over time, as it's really the only thing holding me back right now.

10/07/2026 - 14:59

We live in a simulation

I believe in the universe being a simulation, rather than believing in God.

From an evidence standpoint, neither has enough to prove the other wrong. However, humans have made simulations before, and we've advanced so much in such a short amount of time: in the 1990s home computers struggled to render 3D scenes with a few million polygons, while 30 years later the same home hardware renders scenes with billions of polygons, photorealistic graphics, ray tracing, path tracing, npcs, and so on. The first computer made was around 80 years ago. Who's to say what a civilization a few hundred more years advanced than us can create?

I don't believe in God or any of the "evidence" around there being a God. The topic of Gods are entirely philosophical and all the events associated with them in Christianity, Judaism, Islam, Hinduism all seem to display a supernatural being who defies the laws of the universe (ironic). Since I'm strictly a "man of science", finding reason in stuff like this is hard.

Now simulations. We have the evidence right in front of us. We've made simulations. Are we in a simulation? Not necessarily, but it makes the idea conceivable. We have the power to make little people exist in a fake world. We probably are those little people in someone else's fake world. It's an interesting idea.
