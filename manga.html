MANGA CORNER !!

I was the number 1 manga hater. I was also the number 1 anime hater. I always had a picture of what the average manga/anime viewer looked like, some fat greasy gimp.

However, I really enjoyed One Punch Man, to the point of watching it 4 times. After several years of waiting for season 3, (dear god what have they done) I decided to read the manga. And oh my, it was better than I thought it would be.

Long story short, I started to really like reading pictures from a book. Thus began my romance manga addiction. I never pictured myself as a romantical person, but there's something about romances that can't stop me from reading them. I'm a sucker for seeing two people slowly develop a relationship, that be a friendship or an intimate one.

Perhaps I like romance because I dont have any in my life. Or maybe I'm escaping reality, looking at what "could've been" had I made different choices. (most romance manga are set during high school/university). Whatever the reason is, it doesn't matter. It makes me all giddy and kick my feet up n shit.

The Flowers of Evil

My favourite manga of all time.

Kasuga, a middle school student, is an introverted and unsociable boy who likes reading books. His favourite book, Les Fleurs du mal, is about a human's violent and degenerate base desires and the self-loathing that one feels from having them because having those desires conflicts with one's perception of oneself as "good".

He has a crush on the school's most popular girl. Events lead to him stealing her gym clothes, but unbeknownst to him, another girl, Nakamura, saw him take them. The first part of the story explores the character development of both Kasuga and Nakamura as they spend time together.

The major theme of the book is being young and dealing with puberty and sexual desire before you are really ready for it. Kasuga wants to be seen as a "normal" person, but we the readers and Nakamura already know he's a crazy pervert, after we see him take his crush's gym clothes.

Nakamura, Kasuga's classmate, is a very isolated girl with no friends. From her point of view, she sees everyone as monsters. She blackmails Kasuga into forming a "contract" where he must obey her. She makes him strip and wear the stolen gym clothes, steal underwear, etc. Despite having a one-sided relationship, they begin to enjoy their time together, as both of them are outsiders who recognise the same ugly side in each other.

For the first time in her life, Nakamura sees someone else as a person, not as a monster. It's the first time she doesn't feel alone in being a pervert. The manga tells a story of two broken people who become dependent on each other, for better or for worse.

Now this whole part of the manga up until the end is my absolute favourite out of everything I've read, and it's the reason I adore The Flowers of Evil so much.

They go through a stressful event that forces them to separate. They both move away from their tiny, "trapped in" town to continue their lives. Kasuga meets new people, a new love, and begins to move away from his obsession with Nakamura. But he never fully leaves her behind, and eventually he goes looking for her.

They finally meet again, and share a short but bittersweet moment together, before eventually, Kasuga leaves to return to his new life.

As he leaves, Nakamura tells him to never come back. I interpret it as a goodbye, and it's her way of caring without admitting it. By this point in the story, they have both developed their own separate lives and want to protect what they have. Being around each other is simply going to re-open old wounds that are better off staying closed.

Normally, as the reader who was introduced to the two main characters from the first chapter, you will naturally root for them and hope for them to end up together. However, this is one of those stories where no matter how much you want them to be together, you know they're better off being apart.

Kasuga sees his "Flower of Evil" (that is his dark desires and conflicts) wither away as he grows up and experiences his life.
